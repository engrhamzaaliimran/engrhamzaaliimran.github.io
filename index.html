Research identity

One sensor-intelligence framework across sensing modalities.

The sensing modality changes, but the research logic remains consistent: understand the measurement, preserve informative structure, learn representations that generalise, and design for practical acquisition and deployment.

Applied across two sensing modalities

Wearable inertial signals describe human behaviour; impedance spectra describe chemical interactions. Both are treated as structured physical measurements rather than generic input data.

Wearable and physical signals

Human motion through wearable inertial sensors

Since 2019, I have studied how body-worn accelerometers and gyroscopes can support reliable human-centred intelligence, spanning activity recognition, gait-based affective computing, cross-domain representation transfer, and sleep monitoring.

  • Human activity recognition from accelerometer and gyroscope time series
  • Transfer of motion representations to data-scarce emotion tasks
  • Wearable AIoT systems for sleep–wake monitoring
  • Compact CNN, recurrent, and Transformer-based models for edge use

Chemical and electrical signals

Chemical state through impedance spectroscopy

My doctoral work studies how frequency-dependent complex impedance can reveal richer chemical information than a single resistance measurement, while remaining compatible with compact and cost-aware measurement systems.

  • Interpretable full-spectrum impedance models and compact features
  • Single-gas and mixture analysis
  • Efficient strategies

About

Building sensor intelligence across modalities.

I am a Marie Skłodowska-Curie doctoral researcher at Saarland University. I work on the broader problem of extracting reliable and deployable intelligence from physical sensor signals, with current doctoral research focused on excitation design, frequency-domain impedance information, and model-based representations for MOS gas sensors.

Wearable inertial sensing forms the other major body of this work, developed through human activity recognition, gait-based emotion detection, cross-domain transfer learning, sleep monitoring, and compact edge-oriented neural models. These projects share a common workflow: measurement-aware modelling, reusable representations, rigorous validation, and resource-conscious deployment.
