Research identity

One sensor-intelligence framework across sensing modalities.

The sensing modality changes, but the research logic remains consistent: understand the measurement, preserve informative structure, learn representations that generalise, and design for practical acquisition and deployment.

01 Measure

Acquire informative IMU time series or impedance spectra.

→
02 Model

Expose temporal, spectral, and physical signal structure.

→
03 Learn

Build compact, transferable, and interpretable representations.

→
04 Deploy

Reduce sensing, computation, and instrumentation cost.

Applied across two sensing modalities

Wearable inertial signals describe human behaviour; impedance spectra describe chemical interactions. Both are treated as structured physical measurements rather than generic input data.

Wearable and physical signals

Human motion through wearable inertial sensors

Since 2019, I have studied how body-worn accelerometers and gyroscopes can support reliable human-centred intelligence, spanning activity recognition, gait-based affective computing, cross-domain representation transfer, and sleep monitoring.

  • Human activity recognition from accelerometer and gyroscope time series
  • Transfer of motion representations to data-scarce emotion tasks
  • Wearable AIoT systems for sleep–wake monitoring
  • Compact CNN, recurrent, and Transformer-based models for edge use

Chemical and electrical signals

Chemical state through impedance spectroscopy

My doctoral work studies how frequency-dependent complex impedance can reveal richer chemical information than a single resistance measurement, while remaining compatible with compact and cost-aware measurement systems.

  • Interpretable full-spectrum impedance models and compact features
  • Single-gas and mixture analysis
  • Efficient strategies

HomeMind: from a local LLM to a verifiable smart-home agent

A local Qwen3 smart-home agent using LangChain tool calling and JSON-backed simulated devices. 03 · Implemented Edge-RAG

Portfolio Edge-RAG: validated retrieval with browser-local generation

A static-site RAG chatbot built from 13 curated portfolio sources and 88 heading-aware chunks. A 100-question benchmark compared Dense MiniLM, BM25, and RRF; BM25 was selected, then paired with compact evidence selection, Qwen3-0.6B browser generation, and an extractive fallback. Generation is manually smoke-tested; detailed judge-based generation evaluation remains a future validation task.

About

Building intelligent systems — from embedded software to applied AI.

I am an AI and software engineer with an MSc in Computer Science and a background spanning embedded systems, machine learning, and applied research. Currently, I am a Marie Skłodowska-Curie doctoral researcher at Saarland University, developing intelligent sensing methods that combine experimental measurements, signal processing, and machine learning.

My engineering experience includes embedded Linux, C/C++, Python, IoT systems, and software development. My research has focused on deep learning for wearable inertial sensors and intelligent chemical sensing, with an emphasis on robust models, real-world validation, and efficient deployment.

More recently, I have been expanding my work into Generative AI, including large language models, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), GraphRAG, and agentic systems. I am particularly interested in bringing these technologies together to develop reliable, practical AI applications.
